    Caveats

    Eight problems that apply to the catalogue as a whole. Each programme page lists its own threats; these recur everywhere.

    MethodsTreatment datesCaveats

    District boundaries move, and the change is invisible in the data

    Pakistan created new districts throughout the period this catalogue covers: Nankana Sahib in 2005, Chiniot in 2009, the seven ex-FATA tribal districts in 2018, and five more in Punjab alone in 2022–23 (Kot Addu, Talagang, Murree, Wazirabad, Taunsa). The Bureau of Statistics reissues district codes when this happens.

    What to do. Harmonise to a constant boundary set before building the panel: collapse post-split children into their pre-split parent, and keep the crosswalk as a separate documented file. A silent boundary change looks exactly like a treatment effect, and reviewers will not catch it either.

    Most of these programmes were targeted at districts that were already deteriorating

    Zewar-e-Taleem selected on low female literacy. Nashonuma selected on high stunting. The Public School Support Programme selected on low enrolment. Health facility outsourcing selected on poor performance. This is sensible policy and terrible for identification: it guarantees mean reversion, and the reversion often begins exactly at the treatment date, producing clean pre-trends.

    What to do. Plot the full event-study path, use honest DiD sensitivity analysis, match on baseline levels and trends, and where the targeting rule is a threshold on a measurable variable, exploit it as a discontinuity instead of assuming parallel trends.

    Treatment is frequently not absorbing

    Contracting out of basic health units was reversed and re-awarded. The Saaf Pani programme was suspended during a corruption inquiry and relaunched. Local governments were dissolved and restored more than once. Standard staggered estimators assume once-treated-always-treated.

    What to do. Use an estimator that handles switching in and out, and document every reversal date explicitly rather than coding the programme as a single absorbing event.

    Population composition is endogenous to several of these treatments

    Conflict, floods, new roads and new university campuses all move people between districts. If treatment changes who lives in the district, a district-level estimate confounds the treatment effect with a composition effect, and the bias can go either way.

    What to do. Test for sorting directly using the migration modules in PSLM and the census. Where sorting is present, report it as a result in its own right; where the treatment is large enough to move people, consider the receiving districts as the better-identified side of the question.

    Administrative outcomes often improve because measurement improved

    Reported crime rises when the cost of registering a complaint falls. Teacher presence rises when monitoring digitises. Land transactions rise when the registry becomes usable. In each case part of the measured change is real and part is the measurement system changing at the same moment as the treatment.

    What to do. Pair every administrative outcome with an independent household-survey measure of the same construct where one exists, and treat the gap between them as informative rather than as noise.

    Coverage of this catalogue is uneven by province

    Punjab publishes far more than the other provinces, so Punjab entries are better documented here. Balochistan is the thinnest, both in the catalogue and in the underlying survey infrastructure — several national household surveys have district-representative samples everywhere except there.

    What to do. Do not read the density of entries as the density of policy. Where a Balochistan entry is graded Low-Medium, that usually reflects what is published rather than what happened.

    Dates reported in secondary sources disagree, routinely

    Programme retrospectives, agency websites and press coverage frequently differ by a year on when a phase began, and launch ceremonies are often held well before or after operational start.

    What to do. Every date in this catalogue carries a named source. Verify against the primary document before it enters a regression, and when sources conflict, say in the paper which one you used and why.

    Expected signs here are hypotheses, not findings

    The direction given for each programme–outcome pair is derived from the stated mechanism. Several are deliberately marked ambiguous because theory points both ways. None of them is evidence.

    What to do. Treat the sign column as a prior to be tested, and pre-register it if you are going to. Where the catalogue says ambiguous, resist the temptation to pick the direction your result happens to take.
