    Drone strike campaign

    US drone strike campaign. Strikes concentrated 2008-2014, declining to 2018.

    Overview

    Precise dates and locations, but no household data for the affected areas and severe endogeneity. Best used with high-frequency outcomes (violence, migration into settled districts) rather than welfare outcomes.

    Implementing agency
    External (US)
    Scope
    FATA agencies
    Rollout window
    Strikes concentrated 2008-2014, declining to 2018
    Unit of rollout
    Agency / tehsil, strike-level with dates
    Type of staggering
    Continuous strike intensity with precise dates
    Assignment rule
    Targeting is endogenous to militant presence
    Designs it suits
    Staggered DiD, Continuous treatment

    Rollout

    No district-by-date list has been documented for this entry yet. The map shows its stated scope only.

    Where to get district-by-date data

    New America Foundation, Bureau of Investigative Journalism, Long War Journal strike databases

    Assessed quality: High for strike dates and locations. How to obtain timing data

    Identification

    Main threats

    No survey coverage of FATA pre-merger; targeting endogeneity

    Existing evidence

    Fair et al. on drone strikes and public opinion; Johnston & Sarbahi (2016)

    References

    • Johnston, P. B. and Sarbahi, A. K. (2016). The impact of US drone strikes on terrorism in Pakistan. International Studies Quarterly 60(2), 203–219. Find on Google Scholar (details to verify)

    Data coverage

    Survey waves before, during and after the rollout window (2004–2018, shaded). Waves before the first phase identify pre-trends; waves inside the window carry the staggered comparison.

    Survey waveContinuous seriesRollout window

    Power

    Constant 2019 boundaries in scope
    0.5 suits a not-yet-treated comparison
    AR(1) residual correlation
    —
    minimum detectable effect, in residual standard deviations

    Difference-in-differences variance with serially correlated errors (Burlig, Preonas and Woerman 2020), AR(1) residuals, two-group equivalent. Heterogeneity-robust staggered estimators usually need somewhat more.
