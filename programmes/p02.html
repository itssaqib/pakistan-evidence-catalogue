    BISP Kafaalat cash transfer

    BISP Kafaalat unconditional cash transfer (first payment). 2008-09 parliamentarian phase; scorecard-based enrolment 2010-2016; continuous expansion since.

    Overview

    Two designs stack neatly: (i) staggered district DiD on the first-payment date, (ii) RD at the PMT cut-off within district. The interaction identifies whether the transfer works differently where coverage is dense.

    Implementing agency
    BISP
    Scope
    National
    Rollout window
    2008-09 parliamentarian phase; scorecard-based enrolment 2010-2016; continuous expansion since
    Unit of rollout
    District (first-payment date and coverage intensity)
    Type of staggering
    Continuous phase-in of coverage intensity plus discrete district start dates
    Assignment rule
    PMT score cut-off (16.17, later 32.5 then 37) within districts; district entry follows the census sequence
    Designs it suits
    Staggered DiD, Regression discontinuity, Continuous treatment

    Identification

    Main threats

    General-equilibrium spillovers within districts break SUTVA when coverage is high; inflation eroded the real transfer over time

    Existing evidence

    OPM final evaluation (2016); Haseeb & Vyborny (2022) on payment mechanisms

    Power

    Constant 2019 boundaries in scope
    0.5 suits a not-yet-treated comparison
    AR(1) residual correlation
    —
    minimum detectable effect, in residual standard deviations

    Difference-in-differences variance with serially correlated errors (Burlig, Preonas and Woerman 2020), AR(1) residuals, two-group equivalent. Heterogeneity-robust staggered estimators usually need somewhat more.
