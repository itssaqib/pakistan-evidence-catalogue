Guidance

Identification playbook

Twelve things worth settling before estimating anything with the variation in this catalogue. None of it is novel; all of it is routinely skipped.

Staggered rollout is not a free identification strategy. In this setting it comes with three recurring problems — programmes targeted at districts that were already deteriorating, administrative boundaries that move, and treatments that switch off again — and each one produces a result that looks clean and is wrong.

01

Do not use two-way fixed effects on its own

With variation in treatment timing and any heterogeneity in effects across cohorts, the TWFE coefficient is a weighted average of 2×2 comparisons in which already-treated units serve as controls for later-treated units, and some of those weights are negative. Report the Goodman-Bacon decomposition to show how much of the estimate comes from that forbidden comparison, then estimate with Callaway–Sant'Anna, Sun–Abraham, de Chaisemartin–D'Haultfœuille, or Borusyak–Jaravel–Spiess. Show that they agree; where they do not, say why.

02

Choose the comparison group deliberately

Never-treated and not-yet-treated districts give different estimands. Most large Pakistani programmes eventually reach every district, so the never-treated group is often empty or unusual — typically Balochistan or ex-FATA, which differ on everything. Prefer not-yet-treated comparisons, and say so explicitly rather than letting the software choose.

03

Pre-trends are necessary but not sufficient

Many programmes here were deliberately targeted at districts doing badly: low literacy, high stunting, low enrolment. That guarantees mean reversion, which can look like a treatment effect and can also produce clean-looking pre-trends when the reversion begins exactly at treatment. Plot the full event-study path, run the Rambachan–Roth honest DiD sensitivity analysis, and consider matching on baseline levels and trends before estimating.

04

Cluster and infer carefully

Pakistan has roughly 130–160 districts depending on the year and definition — enough for district-clustered standard errors in most cases. But several entries here have fewer than 20 treated districts (Nashonuma phase 1, Safe Cities, Daanish Schools). For those, use wild cluster bootstrap or randomisation inference, and report the treated-cluster count prominently rather than in a footnote.

05

Get the boundaries right before anything else

Harmonise every district to a constant-boundary definition, usually the 1998 or 2017 census set, and keep the crosswalk as a separate documented file. Ex-FATA needs its own decision: drop it, or treat the seven tribal districts as entering the panel in 2018. Whichever you choose, do it once and state it. A silent boundary change looks exactly like a treatment effect.

06

Watch for migration sorting

District-level estimates assume the population is fixed. Conflict, floods, roads and university openings all move people. If treatment changes who lives in the district, the estimate confounds a treatment effect with a composition effect. Test it directly using the PSLM and census migration modules, and where sorting is present, report it as a finding rather than a caveat.

07

Treatment is often not absorbing

Contracting out of basic health units was reversed and re-awarded. Saaf Pani was suspended mid-programme. Local governments were dissolved and restored. Standard staggered estimators assume an absorbing treatment. Where it is not, use the de Chaisemartin–D'Haultfœuille estimator, which handles switching in and out, and document every reversal date.

08

Distinguish the policy from its intensity

Several entries are better modelled as a continuous dose — lady health worker coverage share, training slots per capita, broadband coverage share, flood inundation share — than as a binary switch. A binary indicator on a continuous treatment throws away most of the variation and usually attenuates the estimate.

09

Combine designs where the assignment rule allows it

Several programmes have a rule-based eligibility threshold inside the district: the BISP proxy means test cut-off, the Punjab Education Foundation's Quality Assurance Test score, the Female School Stipend's 1998 literacy threshold, the NCOC's positivity thresholds. A within-district regression discontinuity alongside the across-district difference-in-differences tests the same hypothesis under different assumptions. Agreement between the two is far more persuasive than either alone.

10

Prefer sub-district treatment units when they exist

Land records digitisation operated at tehsil level across 143 centres; health facility outsourcing and school handovers at facility and school level; filtration plants at plant level; broadband subsidies at project-lot level. Using the finer unit lets you include district × year fixed effects, which absorbs the district-level political and budgetary confounds a pure district design cannot. This is usually the single largest available improvement in credibility.

11

Frequency matters more than people expect

Monthly facility data, quarterly district microfinance penetration, daily mandi prices, monthly nightlights and continuous citizen-feedback call records give far more power than the biennial household surveys, and let you separate anticipation from response. Where a high-frequency administrative outcome exists, make it the primary outcome and use the survey as corroboration.

12

Adjust for multiple hypotheses

This catalogue lists many outcomes per programme by design. If you run all of them, adjust: pre-specify a small set of primary outcomes, group the rest into indices (Anderson sharpened q-values or Romano–Wolf stepdown), and report both adjusted and unadjusted p-values.

Toolkit

Stata packages worth having installed

Equivalents exist in R (did, didimputation, fixest::sunab, HonestDiD, synthdid).

csdid · drdid Callaway–Sant'Anna group-time average treatment effects with doubly robust estimation. ssc install csdid, replace

A minimal starting specification

Replace the placeholders and run the diagnostics before the headline regression, not after.

* 1. Build on constant boundaries first
use district_panel.dta, clear
xtset district_1998 year

* 2. Define cohort = first year treated (missing = never treated)
bysort district_1998: egen gvar = min(cond(treat==1, year, .))
recode gvar (.=0)

* 3. Diagnostic: how much comes from the forbidden comparison?
xtdidregress (y) (treat), group(district_1998) time(year)
bacondecomp y treat, ddetail

* 4. Estimate properly
csdid y, ivar(district_1998) time(year) gvar(gvar) ///
      method(dripw) notyet
estat event, window(-5 5)
csdid_plot

* 5. Sensitivity to violations of parallel trends
honestdid, pre(1/5) post(6/11) mvec(0(0.25)1) coefplot

Reporting checklist

  • State the boundary definition and the year it is fixed to.
  • Report the number of treated clusters, not just the number of observations.
  • Name the comparison group: never-treated, not-yet-treated, or both.
  • Show the Goodman-Bacon decomposition alongside the modern estimator.
  • Plot the full event-study path, including the leads you did not like.
  • Say where the treatment date came from, and how confident you are in it.
  • Test for migration sorting wherever the treatment could move people.
  • Document every treatment reversal; do not quietly treat it as absorbing.
  • Pre-specify primary outcomes; adjust the rest for multiple testing.
