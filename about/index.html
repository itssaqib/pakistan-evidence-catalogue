    About

    What the catalogue is, how it was built, and what it is not.

    Why it exists

    Pakistan ran an unusual number of large public programmes that reached different places at different times. Health insurance activated division by division across Punjab within a year. Cash transfers were gated by the date each district's poverty census finished. Districts were created, merged and renamed throughout.

    That is the variation causal inference needs, and most of it has never been used. The reason is rarely the question. It is that establishing when each district was treated is a research project in itself, and there is no way to know in advance whether an outcome exists at the same resolution.

    The catalogue collects both facts for 67 entries, maps 322 programme–outcome pairs against 55 datasets, and grades how reliable the timing data is, including where it is not reliable at all.

    How it was built

    From agency websites and annual reports, provincial development programme registers, World Bank and ADB project documents, third-party evaluations and the published literature. Rollout lists appear on the map only when a named source lists the districts; everything else is shown as stated scope.

    District boundaries are geoBoundaries' 2019 polygons (public domain), simplified to about one kilometre with shared borders preserved, and projected in an equal-area projection.

    What it is not

    It is not a census of Pakistani public policy: Punjab is documented far better than Balochistan. It is not a store of treatment-timing data, except where districts are listed with sources. And it is not evidence: the expected directions are hypotheses, and nothing here has been estimated.

    Changes in version 2.0

    • District maps for every entry, with 13 documented rollout footprints and their sources.
    • District crosswalk and lineage table on constant 2019 boundaries.
    • Stata starter kit, per-programme do-files, .dta exports and a power calculator.
    • Evidence status, related entries and dataset panel-readiness for every record.
    • P14 KP Sehat Card Plus. v2.0: pilot districts named. Source: Dawn, 16 December 2015.
    • P33 Punjab Skills Development Fund. v2.0 correction: v1.0 listed Rahim Yar Khan instead of Muzaffargarh and dated the start to 2010. Source: PSDF Newsletter 2012, Vol. I.
    • P40 Punjab Safe Cities. v2.0 correction: v1.0 omitted Kasur. Source: Punjab Police press release, 2 January 2019.
